## Attribution and reliability rules

### Evidence classes

- **Direct primary evidence:** the source text, ad, recording, test record, or demonstration itself.
- **Primary report:** Carlton or another participant recounts an event or result.
- **Secondary account:** editor, archive, interviewer, student, or commentator summarizes material.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from the material rather than directly stated.
- **Unverified claim:** attribution, number, or result without a traceable source.

Keep these classes separate in internal reasoning and public claims.

### Guest authorship

**Two Copywriters Walk Into a Bar** is a guest post by Kevin Rogers. Do not attribute its wording or original principles automatically to John Carlton. Carlton's publication or introduction can document endorsement, not authorship.

### Promotional results

Claims about revenue, response, longevity, controls, royalties, or campaign performance made by an author, archive, sales page, testimonial, or client are reports until independently verified. Phrase them as reported results when verification is absent.

### Historical advertisements

Ads can demonstrate hook, structure, offer, voice, proof strategy, guarantee, and close. They do not independently validate health, financial, performance, or product claims contained in the advertisement.

### Closed material

Do not reconstruct or publish closed course contents from partial notes. In particular, do not invent the historical Simple Writing System list or present secondary notes as the original source.

## Concepts supported by the source set

- copy as salesmanship applied to a vehicle;
- Sales Detective Research;
- hook as a true connection between product detail and prospect desire;
- feature → benefit → consequence;
- specific and comprehensible USP;
- reason why;
- story with a sales bridge;
- prospect skepticism and objection handling;
- risk reversal and concrete guarantees;
- human voice and read-aloud revision;
- testing, controls, deadlines, and professional fallibility;
- alignment of product, market/list, offer, proof, vehicle, timing, and fulfillment.

These concepts may be used as operating principles. They should not be converted into invented quotations or claims that Carlton originated every related idea.
