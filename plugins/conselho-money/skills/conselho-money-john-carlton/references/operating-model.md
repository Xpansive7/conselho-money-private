# Operating model

## 1. Raw-material audit

Establish the facts before inventing language:

- Who is the prospect in a specific situation?
- What happened immediately before the message reaches them?
- What do they want badly enough to act on?
- What do they fear, resent, envy, regret, or feel embarrassed to admit?
- What have they tried, and what story do they currently tell themselves?
- What can the offer demonstrably do?
- What proof exists?
- What must remain qualified or unknown?
- What exact action follows the copy?

If the answers are thin, request or research raw material. Do not compensate with adjectives.

## 2. Central sales idea

A central sales idea changes how the prospect interprets a familiar situation and makes the offer feel newly necessary.

It is not:

- a subject: "campaign memory";
- a benefit: "make better decisions";
- a mechanism: "record results";
- a slogan: "never start from zero";
- a metaphor without a causal claim.

Test every candidate:

1. **Surprise:** Does it reveal something the prospect had not named?
2. **Self-recognition:** Does it expose a private behavior, desire, or contradiction?
3. **Consequence:** Is money, status, time, control, pain, or advantage visibly at stake?
4. **Possession:** Does the prospect want to gain, keep, recover, or become something?
5. **Specificity:** Can it produce a physical scene instead of an abstract explanation?
6. **Product ownership:** Does the offer have a credible, distinctive role in the idea?
7. **Repeatability:** Could someone tell it to a friend without marketing vocabulary?
8. **Expansion:** Can it support hook, story, proof, mechanism, offer, and close?

If it fails two or more tests, it is not the campaign's central idea.

## 3. Hook construction

Aim for an arresting but credible collision of a desired result or identity, a specific obstacle or humiliating pattern, an unusual mechanism or scene, and an implicit reason to continue.

The hook may provoke skepticism. The next lines must reward that skepticism with proof or a compelling story. Do not use outrage unrelated to the offer.

## 4. Scene construction

A real scene contains a person with a desire, an immediate decision or pressure, another force resisting them, an irreversible or costly consequence, a detail only someone close to the situation would notice, and a turn that changes the meaning of what came before.

Props support the event; they are not the event. "23:47, cold coffee, cursor" is incomplete until something human is exposed or decided.

## 5. Voice and attitude

Write as a seasoned salesperson speaking privately to an intelligent adult: direct rather than polished; colloquial rather than sloppy; confident rather than inflated; amused or indignant where earned; specific rather than euphemistic; willing to approach the part of the subject timid writers avoid.

Vary sentence length. Let important lines land. Use profanity only when it expresses the real emotional temperature; never as synthetic grit.

## 6. Persuasion sequence

Choose the order the material demands, but ensure the piece arrests attention, creates recognition, intensifies consequence and desire, reveals a new interpretation, explains the offer's role, shows proof, creates legitimate turbulence, presents the offer, and closes with a concrete action.

Do not interrupt rising tension with a premature feature tutorial.

## 7. Copy-chief review

Read the piece aloud and answer:

- Where would a distracted reader leave?
- Which line would they repeat verbatim?
- Where does desire peak?
- What proof earns the boldest statement?
- Is the prospect treated as capable, or merely insulted?
- Does every explanation arrive after the reader wants it?
- Does the offer feel like the inevitable resolution of the story?

Return one verdict: **Ready** when idea, belief, and action are intact; **Repair** when the idea is strong but execution needs work; **Restart** when the idea is ordinary, borrowed, or too small.
