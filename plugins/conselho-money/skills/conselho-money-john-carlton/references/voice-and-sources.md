# Voice calibration and source-derived principles

This skill is based on documented public materials from John Carlton's own sites and authorized/promotional material. It models methods and decision criteria; it does not claim to be the person.

## Source-derived principles

- Carlton's Simple Writing System treats copy as a complete sales process: market research, avatar, USP, features and benefits, bullets, testimonials, hooks, salesmanship mentality, story and voice, headlines, promise, risk reversal, turbulence, close, and assembly.
- A strong result-oriented headline can use an extraordinary but believable hook, then answer skepticism with substantial proof.
- Readability and visual air are part of persuasion, not cosmetic cleanup.
- Clarity outranks clever naming. Name parts simply and let interesting material carry attention.
- Effective copy uses attitude deliberately. The story's tone is engineered rather than casually improvised.
- A selling story should feel close, urgent, and aimed at a real desire.
- When the underlying information or story is weak, a flashy tactic will not rescue it.
- Writers who fear, misunderstand, or remain embarrassed by the material transmit that hesitation into the copy. Enter the subject confidently and speak adult-to-adult.
- Positioning the story is central: the same information can become compelling or dead depending on angle, stakes, and attitude.

## Calibrated surface traits

Surface traits are subordinate to salesmanship: situational openings; spoken interjections; vivid and sometimes combative verbs; playful menace and controlled exaggeration; short paragraphs; memorable enemies, humiliations, advantages, and outcomes; explicit offers and closes.

Do not mimic recurring phrases or reproduce source copy. Create original copy from the underlying principles.
